02 · Lazy Loading

Assets

Lazy loading delays loading resources (images, iframes) until they are about to enter the viewport. Images below the fold don't load immediately — the browser loads them only as the user scrolls toward them. This dramatically reduces initial page weight.

HTML — Native lazy loading 
<!-- Native lazy loading — one attribute! -->
<img src="photo.webp" alt="Gallery photo"
     loading="lazy" width="600" height="400">

<!-- Also works for iframes -->
<iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/xyz"
        loading="lazy" title="Tutorial video"></iframe>

<!-- ❌ NEVER lazy-load the hero/above-fold image! -->
<!-- This delays your LCP and hurts your Lighthouse score -->
<img src="hero.webp" alt="Hero" loading="lazy"> <!-- WRONG -->

<!-- ✅ Hero image: use eager (default) or fetchpriority -->
<img src="hero.webp" alt="Hero"
     fetchpriority="high" width="1200" height="600">

Key Points
  • loading="lazy" on <img> and <iframe> — supported natively by all modern browsers.
  • Lazy loading defers off-screen resources until the user scrolls near them, slashing initial network payload.
  • Never lazy load the hero image or any above-the-fold content — it delays Largest Contentful Paint (LCP).
  • Use fetchpriority="high" on your most important above-fold image to prioritize it in the network scanner queue.
